Who this is for

Read this first, so you know whether the course fits you and what it expects. New to CA? Take the Learn CA from scratch route.

Who it is for

  • M&E officers and evaluators who want to run a Contribution Analysis
  • Commissioners and reviewers who need to judge one
  • Students and researchers learning theory-based evaluation

What you need before you start

  • Some experience with a theory of change, from building one or working under one
  • Familiarity with basic evaluation terms such as outcome, indicator and assumption
  • No background in Contribution Analysis
  • A web browser. Nothing to install or buy

What the course asks of you

  • Eight lessons of about 1,000 to 1,300 words, each meant for one sitting
  • In each lesson, a figure to try, five scenario questions at master’s level and a writing task with a model answer
  • From lesson 2 on, a short recall of the lesson before
  • Statements about the method cite a page, so you can check them at the source
Why it is free

Much of the Contribution Analysis literature sits behind journal paywalls, and what is free is scattered across many sites. This course puts the method in one structured place, free to use and adapt under CC BY 4.0.

Questions

Do I need to know Contribution Analysis already?

No. Some experience with a theory of change helps. Lesson 1 starts from what a contribution claim says, and each later lesson builds on the one before.

How were the sources checked?

Each source enters the bibliography only after its title, year and pages are confirmed at the publisher, a repository or Crossref. Sources that cannot yet be confirmed wait in a verification queue. Citation details that differ between published reference lists are recorded on the errata page, with the version used here and why.

Can I use it offline?

Yes. On GitHub, choose Code, then Download ZIP. Unzip the folder and open any lesson in your browser. Keep the folder structure as it is, so each page finds its stylesheet and scripts.

Can I reuse it in my own training?

Yes. Written material is under CC BY 4.0, so you may share and adapt it, including commercially, if you give credit. Code is under MIT. Material quoted from other authors stays under its own licence.

How do I cite it?

Wenareeba, Morris. (2026). Contribution Analysis: Course, Bibliography, Templates and Red Team Protocol. GitHub repository. GitHub’s “Cite this repository” button gives the same entry from the citation file.

How do I report an error or suggest a source?

Open an issue with one of the forms. Every issue gets a first reply within seven days, and confirmed citation errors are corrected within fourteen days.

Help keep it accurate

Found a citation error, or know a published Contribution Analysis from Africa, Asia or Latin America? Those are the most useful additions.
