# Contribution claim checklist

Run this on every contribution claim before it goes into a report.

**Claim as drafted:**

> Part 1. The intervention contributed to ...
> Part 2. It did so by ...

- [ ] Names a specific result at a named level of the ToC
- [ ] Says "contributed", not "caused", "drove" or "was responsible for"
- [ ] Part 2 gives the mechanism step by step, and names the support factors (causal link assumptions) that had to hold
- [ ] Matches the question type: no ranking or "main driver" language unless the question asked for it (Mayne 2019, p. 176)
- [ ] Rests on evidence that the results and assumptions happened, not on stakeholders saying the programme worked (p. 175)
- [ ] Any statement that the intervention was a causal factor rests on more than hoop tests (p. 186)
- [ ] No figure for the size or share of the contribution unless another method supports it (pp. 181, 187)
- [ ] States confidence, and the confidence matches the tests passed
- [ ] A claim about a distant outcome rests on credible claims for the earlier links (p. 182)
- [ ] Rests on evidence that the activities were delivered as the ToC sets out
- [ ] Says for whom and where the claim holds; any wider claim is argued (p. 186)

Sources: Mayne, J. (2019). Revisiting contribution analysis. *Canadian Journal of Program Evaluation*, 34(2), 171 to 191. https://doi.org/10.3138/cjpe.68004. The implementation check follows the conditions for a sufficient contribution story in Apgar and Levine (2024), BetterEvaluation: https://www.betterevaluation.org/methods-approaches/approaches/contribution-analysis
