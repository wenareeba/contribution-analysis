Contribution analysis · Reference card 1

CA core card

The essentials from Lesson 1. Page numbers refer to Mayne (2019), CJPE 34(2), 171 to 191, unless stated.

Contributory cause

A necessary or likely necessary member of a causal package that, together, was sufficient for the change (p. 174). Each member is an INUS condition (Mackie 1974; p. 187 n. 3). Members are logically equal, so CA does not rank by share.

Two-part claim (p. 182)

  1. The intervention contributed to the observed change.
  2. It did so in the following manner: the causal narrative.

Necessity grounds part 1; a sufficient package grounds part 2.

Steps (Box 1, p. 173)

  1. Set out the cause-effect questions
  2. Develop "robust" theories of change (Mayne's term: ones that can carry a claim)
  3. Gather existing evidence on results and assumptions
  4. Assemble and assess the claim and challenges to it
  5. Seek additional evidence
  6. Revise and strengthen the claim
  7. Return to step 4 if necessary

Earlier statements (e.g. Mayne 2008) give six steps. Box 1 in 2019 makes the return to step 4 an explicit seventh step.

Question decides scope (pp. 175 to 176)

  • Contribution question (did it contribute, and how): other factors need not play a major role, unless causally linked to the package.
  • Causes-of-outcome question (what brought about the outcome): other factors must be explored.
  • Relative importance: compare factors after defining "importance" (p. 181).

Mayne retracted his 2011 "show external factors did not contribute" and 2012 "explore rivals" statements (p. 175). External factors are usually other causal factors, not rivals.

Evidence tools (Table 1, p. 184)

  • Verify the ToC: results and assumptions happened
  • Hoop: plausible time sequence
  • Hoop: reasonable effort expended
  • Hoop: expected effects seen
  • Narrative: the package was sufficient
  • Smoking gun: unique effects seen

What CA cannot do

  • Estimate how much of an outcome is due to the intervention (pp. 181, 187)
  • Rest a claim on stakeholder opinion about the contribution (p. 175)
  • Work from a weak ToC: weak ToC, weak claim (p. 176)

Check question fit

A contribution question closed with a causes-of-outcome or ranking claim answers a bigger question than the one set (p. 176). Check whether the design set out to answer it.
