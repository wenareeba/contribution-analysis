    07 / 08Contribution Analysis

    Critiques and responses

    A method you cannot defend is a method you should not use. This lesson sets out the published criticisms of CA, the replies to them, and what each means for the next report you write. Four short sections, one exercise to try, five questions and one reviewer to answer.

    On this page
      By the end of this lesson

      You can state the main published criticisms of CA and the responses to them, and use them to strengthen how you run and report a CA.

      01The criticisms fall into four groups

      Each criticism below is given in its author's terms, with the reply or the practical implication beside it. Choose a group.

      • No accepted standards, benchmarks or quality criteriaBudhwani and McDavid 2017, p. 8Implication. Echoed by later reviews calling for standards and reporting guidelines (Buetti et al. 2025; Bohni Nielsen and Lemire 2026).
      • A tendency to find some contributionBudhwani and McDavid 2017, p. 9Mayne's reply. With a sound theory of change confirmed by evidence, the likelihood of bias is greatly reduced (2019, p. 177).
      • The "reasonable person" test is vagueBudhwani and McDavid 2017, pp. 9 to 10Implication. Make the evidence threshold explicit for each link, before the evidence is in.
      • Judgements of evidence are subjectiveBudhwani and McDavid 2017, p. 10Their proposal. Use process tracing tests or Bayesian reasoning to judge evidence (p. 13; see also Befani and Mayne 2014).
      • Claims rest on stakeholders believing the programme workedSchmitt and Beach 2015, as quoted by Mayne 2019, p. 175Mayne's reply. Basing claims on opinions about them "is not part of the process"; reports relying solely on opinion "are not reporting on a CA" (p. 175).
      • Theories of change "hide" the mechanism at workSchmitt and Beach 2015, as reported by Mayne 2019, p. 185Mayne's reply. In a theory of change approach, context and mechanisms are captured by the causal link assumptions (p. 185).
      • Causality is mostly linear, and "contributory cause" lumps very different roles togetherSridharan, Nakaima and Pereira 2023, pp. 376, 379 to 381Implication. Describe the specific role each factor played, not just its label. Mayne reports nested theories of change, which unpack a complex intervention and its context, working well in numerous situations (2019, p. 178).
      • Resource intensiveBudhwani and McDavid 2017, pp. 10 to 11Implication. Scope tightly and aim new data at the weak links (Lesson 6; Hopkins 2021).
      • Too often "nothing more than a label of rigor"Delahais and Toulemonde 2017, p. 371Implication. Show each step and the evidence for each link, so a reader can see that CA was actually done.
      • Less precise and testable than related approachesLeeuw 2023, p. 414Implication. Make the theory of change's claims specific enough that evidence could contradict them.
      • Risk of improving the narrative rather than the policyLeeuw 2023, p. 415Implication. Test the theory, not just the story, and use what evaluations of similar interventions found, which CA does not explicitly do (p. 416).

      02Budhwani and McDavid propose six ways to raise rigour

      Their adaptations (2017, pp. 12 to 14):

      • searching for alternative explanations and influencing factors before collecting data, using the Relevant Explanation Finder (p. 12);
      • focusing on the weakest links, starting from a default that the programme did not contribute;
      • process tracing or Bayesian reasoning to judge evidence;
      • triangulating methods and evidence;
      • using stakeholders as a jury on the evidence;
      • applying Patton's rigour criteria.

      Mayne disagreed with the first of these: how far other factors need exploring depends on the question asked (2019, pp. 175 to 176). Lesson 4 sets out both sides.

      03Answer the critique, not the critic

      Four criticisms a reviewer might make of a CA report. Choose the response that actually answers each one.

      Pick the strongest response

      "Your claim rests on programme staff saying the programme was decisive."

      "CA always finds some contribution."

      "Your judgement of the evidence is subjective."

      "Your theory of change hides the mechanism."

      04Write your next report so the critiques do not land

      • State your evidence threshold for each link before you look at the evidence.
      • Look for evidence that could disconfirm the claim, not just evidence that supports it.
      • Treat views that the programme "made the difference" as claims to test, not as evidence.
      • Explain the mechanism at each key link in plain terms.
      • Report your methods fully enough for a reader to retrace them.

      PracticeAnswer a peer reviewer

      The review comment "Your contribution claim rests on interviews with programme staff and partners who all say the programme was decisive. This is opinion, not evidence." Write a short response that accepts or rebuts the point, and says what you would change.

      We accept the point where it applies. Statements that the programme was decisive are opinions about the contribution, and a CA does not rest claims on them (Mayne 2019, p. 175); we have removed them from the evidence base. The interviews remain useful as evidence that specific results and assumptions occurred, for example that district officers received the briefing notes in March. We have strengthened the key link with documentary evidence: the revised guideline reproduces two recommendations that appear only in the programme's briefing. That is a unique effect, which works as a smoking gun (Mayne 2019, p. 184).
