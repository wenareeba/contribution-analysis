1. Why this is the most debated part of CA

Almost every intervention works alongside other things: other programmes, prices, policies, the weather. How far an evaluator has to examine them is where much of the published debate on CA sits. This lesson sets out what the main authors say, in their own terms.

2. Mayne's position changed

WhenPositionSource
2011A contribution claim could be made once external factors were shown not to have contributed significantlyAs Mayne reports it (2019, p. 175)
2012Rival explanations need to be exploredAs Mayne reports it (2019, p. 175)
2019Both statements were incorrect. External factors are usually not rivals but other causal factors at work; the rival label is inappropriate in complex causalityMayne 2019, p. 175

His 2019 rule: whether and how the intervention contributed can be explored without examining factors outside the causal package, unless they are causally linked to it. How far to go depends on the question. For "what brought about the impact?", explore the other factors. For "did the intervention contribute, and how?", they need not play a major role (pp. 175 to 176).

3. Other authors see a bigger role

Lemire, Nielsen and Dybdal (2012) built the Relevant Explanation Finder (REF) to identify and weigh influencing factors and alternative explanations. Budhwani and McDavid (2017, Table 1, p. 12) summarise its criteria: a description of the factor, its type, its level, identifiers that would prove or disprove it, its degree of influence, and its implication, with sub-criteria including certainty, robustness, range, prevalence and theoretical grounding.

Budhwani and McDavid (2017) describe alternative explanations as standing in for counterfactuals when testing a ToC (p. 4), and recommend searching for them systematically before collecting data (p. 12).

Mayne (2019, p. 176) disagreed with both on this point, and notes that Lemire and colleagues themselves concede in a footnote that alternatives are needed only when comparing the relative contribution of the intervention.

Ton et al. (2019, p. 6) add a practical limit: no evaluation can test every alternative explanation, or spend equal effort on each.

4. Inside or outside the package

Mayne's own distinction is the one to start from (2019, pp. 174 to 175):

Where the factor sitsWhat it isWhat to do
Inside the causal packageA support factor: a condition captured by a causal link assumption, which had to hold for the intervention to workVerify it as part of the theory of change and the causal narrative
Outside the package, causally linkedAn external factor that affects the package itselfExamine it, whatever the question
Outside the package, not linkedAnother causal factor at work on the outcomeExamine it if the question asks what caused the outcome; otherwise it need not play a major role

5. Judging the evidence on a factor

The process tracing logic from Lesson 3 applies. Under Collier's scheme (2011, p. 825), a passed smoking gun substantially weakens rival hypotheses, and only doubly decisive evidence eliminates them. Most evidence on other factors is weaker than that, so say plainly how strong it is.

Practice: build an other factors register

Case (invented). A girls' education programme in Arua offers mentoring clubs. Attendance rose. In the same period, the government abolished a school fee, a new road made the school easier to reach, and a radio campaign promoted girls' schooling. The evaluation asks whether the clubs contributed to higher attendance, and how. For each factor, say what role it could play and whether this question requires examining it.

Fee abolition. Plausibly inside the package as a support factor: families need to afford school for mentoring to matter. Verify it as an assumption of the clubs' pathway.
New road. Likely outside the package and not linked: another causal factor at work. For this contribution question it need not play a major role, unless club attendance itself depended on the road.
Radio campaign. Acts on the same attitudes the clubs target, so it is causally linked to the clubs' pathway at the attitude link. Examine it there, for example by asking girls where they heard the messages.
