1. The steps, as an iterative cycle

BetterEvaluation presents Mayne's six steps as an iterative cycle, with a diagram from Apgar, Hernandez and Ton (2020): set out the attribution problem; develop the theory of change and risks to it; gather existing evidence; assemble and assess the contribution story and challenges to it; seek out additional evidence; revise and strengthen the story. Step 6 feeds back into steps 4 and 2 (Apgar and Levine 2024).

Mayne's 2019 version adds the return to step 4 as an explicit seventh step (Box 1, p. 173). Wimbush, Montague and Mulherin (2012) added a different seventh step: using CA to support organisations or networks to review and improve their strategies (Apgar and Levine 2024).

The iteration is the point. Existing evidence comes first; new data collection is aimed at the weak links it reveals.

3. What practitioners report

A scoping review of CA in health found seven studies (Buetti et al. 2025). The patterns are useful for any sector:

  • All seven studies said CA is time-consuming and resource-intensive. One took seven months and would have gone better with eight to ten.
  • Judgement calls were hard: what level of evidence is suitable, and which external factors to include.
  • Practical problems included stakeholders replaced after elections and slow access to government data. Six of the seven built their ToC retrospectively.
  • Benefits: a flexible approach, actionable information for stakeholders, and a common framework and vocabulary.

The authors suggest that standardised reporting guidelines or protocols could improve rigour, transparency and credibility.

4. A worked example to study

BetterEvaluation's example is Delahais and Toulemonde's (2017) evaluation of two forestry research centres' contribution to sustainable forest management in the Congo Basin. The ToC had five contribution pathways. The team tested it with 65 stakeholder interviews, a review of 130 documents and three case studies. They checked the credibility of the revised story three ways: the steering group reviewed all working documents, several researchers reviewed the first case study, and a reviewer examined the credibility of the key findings (Apgar and Levine 2024).

Practice: scope a real CA

You have eight weeks and two people to assess whether a district water programme contributed to fewer school days lost to diarrhoea. Write a five-line scoping note: the result of interest, the question type, which links you will focus new data collection on, your evidence threshold for one key link, and how you will share emerging findings.

Result. School days lost to diarrhoea among pupils in the 12 programme schools, 2024 to 2026.
Question type. Contribution: did the programme contribute, and how?
Focus links. Existing records cover construction; new data targets the weakest links, from stations built to pupils washing hands, and from handwashing to fewer absences.
Threshold. For the handwashing link: spot checks in at least six schools on two unannounced days, plus pupil and teacher accounts that agree.
Sharing. A short findings note to the district team at week five, before the draft report.
