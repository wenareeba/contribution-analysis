## Where to start

1. Mayne (2019a), *Revisiting contribution analysis*. The current statement of the method.
2. Mayne (2008), ILAC Brief 16. Four pages; the version most people cite.
3. Apgar and Levine (2024), BetterEvaluation. Free, practical overview.
4. Delahais and Toulemonde (2017). A full CA done well, start to finish.
5. Bohni Nielsen and Lemire (2026). Where the field stands now.

## 1. Foundations: Mayne's own work

Read in date order to see how the method, and Mayne's view of alternative explanations, changed.

- Mayne, J. (2001). Addressing attribution through contribution analysis: Using performance measures sensibly. *Canadian Journal of Program Evaluation*, 16(1), 1 to 24. https://doi.org/10.3138/cjpe.016.001  
  The journal version. CA as a way to say something credible about attribution from performance data. Open access.
- Mayne, J. (2004). Reporting on outcomes: Setting performance expectations and telling performance stories. *Canadian Journal of Program Evaluation*, 19(1), 31 to 60. https://ces.journals.uvic.ca/index.php/cjpe/article/view/1036  
  The performance story idea that the contribution story grows out of. Open access.
- Mayne, J. (2008). *Contribution analysis: An approach to exploring cause and effect*. ILAC Brief 16, Institutional Learning and Change Initiative. https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/52525/files/ILAC_Brief16_Contribution_Analysis.pdf  
  The most cited short statement: six steps and the conditions for a contribution claim. It is Brief 16, not 15.
- Mayne, J. (2011). Contribution analysis: Addressing cause and effect. In *Evaluating the complex: Attribution, contribution, and beyond (K. Forss, M. Marra and R. Schwartz, Eds.), Transaction Publishers (reissued by Routledge)* (pp. 53 to 96).  
  Chapter 3. Longer treatment for complex settings. Where Mayne said external factors should be shown not to have contributed significantly, a position he withdrew in 2019. Chapter title confirmed in the publisher's contents; page range from Mayne (2019).
- Mayne, J. (2012a). Contribution analysis: Coming of age? *Evaluation*, 18(3), 270 to 280. https://doi.org/10.1177/1356389012451663  
  Opens the 2012 special issue. Contributory causes, INUS conditions, and a call to explore rival explanations that he later withdrew.
- Mayne, J. (2012b). *Making causal claims*. ILAC Brief 26, Institutional Learning and Change Initiative.  
  Short brief on what a causal claim in CA can say.
- Mayne, J. (2015). Useful theory of change models. *Canadian Journal of Program Evaluation*, 30(2), 119 to 142. https://doi.org/10.3138/cjpe.230  
  Narrative, overview and nested ToCs, and causal link assumptions. The DOI is cjpe.230; see errata.
- Mayne, J. (2017). Theory of change analysis: Building robust theories of change. *Canadian Journal of Program Evaluation*, 32(2), 155 to 173. https://doi.org/10.3138/cjpe.31122  
  Criteria for judging whether a ToC can carry a contribution claim. A French version (Théories du changement) appears in the same issue, pp. 174 to 201.
- Mayne, J. (2019a). Revisiting contribution analysis. *Canadian Journal of Program Evaluation*, 34(2), 171 to 191. https://doi.org/10.3138/cjpe.68004  
  Start here. Contributory cause, two-part claims, the retraction on rival explanations (p. 175), Table 1 evidence tools (p. 184), generalising. Open access.
- Mayne, J. (2019b). *Assessing the relative importance of causal factors*. CDI Practice Paper 21, Centre for Development Impact, Institute of Development Studies.  
  How to define and judge relative importance when CA alone cannot size contributions.
- Mayne, J. (2019c). *A brief on contribution analysis: Principles and concepts*. Unpublished brief, ResearchGate. https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331159324  
  Late plain-language summary, including criteria for a good ToC. Confirmed through BetterEvaluation's 'Remembering John Mayne' page, which quotes it.
- Mayne, J. (2020). Sustainability analysis of intervention benefits: A theory of change approach. *Canadian Journal of Program Evaluation*, 35(2), 204 to 221. https://ces.journals.uvic.ca/index.php/cjpe/article/view/249  
  Extends ToC reasoning to whether benefits last. Open access.
- Mayne, J. (2023). Assumptions in theories of change. *Evaluation and Program Planning*, 98, 102276. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.evalprogplan.2023.102276  
  His last word on causal link assumptions, the support factors a contribution claim depends on.

## 2. Method development and debate

Critiques, refinements and combinations with other methods.

- Dybdal, L., Nielsen, S. B., and Lemire, S. (2010). Contribution analysis applied: Reflections on scope and methodology. *Canadian Journal of Program Evaluation*, 25(2), 29 to 57. https://doi.org/10.3138/cjpe.25.002  
  First critical review; few real applications at the time. The journal site misspells the first author as Dybal.
- Lemire, S. T., Nielsen, S. B., and Dybdal, L. (2012). Making contribution analysis work: A practical framework for handling influencing factors and alternative explanations. *Evaluation*, 18(3), 294 to 309. https://doi.org/10.1177/1356389012450654  
  The Relevant Explanation Finder for rating other factors. Mayne (2019, p. 176) disputes that alternatives must be examined.
- Leeuw, F. L. (2012). Linking theory-based evaluation and contribution analysis: Three problems and a few solutions. *Evaluation*, 18(3), 348 to 363. https://doi.org/10.1177/1356389012452051  
  Where CA sits in the theory-based family, and three weaknesses.
- Patton, M. Q. (2012). A utilization-focused approach to contribution analysis. *Evaluation*, 18(3), 364 to 377. https://doi.org/10.1177/1356389012449523  
  CA framed around intended users and use.
- Sridharan, S., and Nakaima, A. (2012). Towards an evidence base of theory-driven evaluations: Some questions for proponents of theory-driven evaluation. *Evaluation*, 18(3), 378 to 395. https://doi.org/10.1177/1356389012453289  
  Hard questions for theory-driven approaches, CA included.
- Vaessen, J., and Raimondo, E. (2012). Making sense of impact: A methodological framework for assessing the impact of prizes. *Evaluation*, 18(3), 330 to 347. https://doi.org/10.1177/1356389012450655  
  A CA-informed framework applied to prizes.
- Collier, D. (2011). Understanding process tracing. *PS: Political Science & Politics*, 44(4), 823 to 830. https://doi.org/10.1017/S1049096511001429  
  Table 1 (p. 825) sets out the four process tracing tests by necessity and sufficiency: straw-in-the-wind, hoop, smoking gun and doubly decisive.
- Pawson, R., and Tilley, N. (2004). *Realist evaluation*. Paper funded by the British Cabinet Office.  
  A short account of realist evaluation, including the weeding out of alternative theories of how a programme works (p. 17).
- Befani, B., and Mayne, J. (2014). Process tracing and contribution analysis: A combined approach to generative causal inference for impact evaluation. *IDS Bulletin*, 45(6), 17 to 36. https://doi.org/10.1111/1759-5436.12110  
  How process tracing tests strengthen step 5 and judge confidence in a claim.
- Schmitt, J., and Beach, D. (2015). The contribution of process tracing to theory-based evaluations of complex aid instruments. *Evaluation*, 21(4), 429 to 447. https://doi.org/10.1177/1356389015607739  
  The critique that CA leans on stakeholder opinion and hides mechanisms. Mayne replies in 2019 (pp. 175, 185).
- Befani, B., Rees, C., Varga, L., and Hills, D. (2016). *Testing contribution claims with Bayesian updating*. CECAN Evaluation and Policy Practice Note (EPPN) No. 2.1, Centre for the Evaluation of Complexity Across the Nexus. https://doi.org/10.15126/00850612  
  Short practical note on Bayesian updating for contribution claims.
- Budhwani, S., and McDavid, J. C. (2017). Contribution analysis: Theoretical and practical challenges and prospects for evaluators. *Canadian Journal of Program Evaluation*, 32(1), 1 to 24. https://doi.org/10.3138/cjpe.31121  
  Critical review: bias, sizing contribution, reliance on alternative explanations.
- Befani, B., and Stedman-Bryce, G. (2017). Process tracing and Bayesian updating for impact evaluation. *Evaluation*, 23(1), 42 to 60. https://doi.org/10.1177/1356389016654584  
  Contribution tracing: process tracing plus Bayesian updating. Online 2016, issue 2017.
- Brousselle, A., and Buregeya, J. M. (2018). Theory-based evaluations: Framing the existence of a new theory in evaluation and the rise of the 5th generation. *Evaluation*, 24(2), 153 to 168. https://doi.org/10.1177/1356389018765487  
  Places CA in a history of theory-based evaluation.
- Lemire, S., Whynot, J., and Montague, S. (2019). How we model matters: A manifesto for the next generation of program theorizing. *Canadian Journal of Program Evaluation*, 33(3), 414 to 433. https://doi.org/10.3138/cjpe.53070  
  On how ToC models are drawn and used; relevant to step 2.
- Dinh, K., Worth, H., Haire, B., and Hong, K. T. (2019). Confucian evaluation: Reframing contribution analysis using a Confucian lens. *American Journal of Evaluation*, 40(4), 562 to 574. https://doi.org/10.1177/1098214018813008  
  CA adapted to a Confucian cultural frame. Useful for thinking about culturally grounded practice.
- Ton, G., Mayne, J., Delahais, T., Morell, J., Befani, B., Apgar, M., and O'Flynn, P. (2019). *Contribution analysis and estimating the size of effects: Can we reconcile the possible with the impossible?* CDI Practice Paper 20, Centre for Development Impact, Institute of Development Studies. https://opendocs.ids.ac.uk  
  Seven authors on whether and how CA can say anything about size.
- Palenberg, M. A. (2023). Causal claims in contribution analysis. *Canadian Journal of Program Evaluation*, 37(3), 389 to 402. https://doi.org/10.3138/cjpe.75428  
  What kind of causal claim CA can and cannot make.
- Leeuw, F. L. (2023). John Mayne and rules of thumb for contribution analysis: A comparison with two related approaches. *Canadian Journal of Program Evaluation*, 37(3), 403 to 421. https://doi.org/10.3138/cjpe.75448  
  CA beside realist evaluation and the policy-scientific approach.
- Sridharan, S., Nakaima, A., and Pereira, A. (2023). Causality and complexity in evaluating equity interventions: Conceptual issues that need to be addressed in theory-driven evaluation approaches. *Canadian Journal of Program Evaluation*, 37(3), 371 to 388. https://doi.org/10.3138/cjpe.75430  
  Equity and complexity questions for CA and its relatives.
- Delahais, T. (2024). Considerations in using theories of change to establish causality: Setting the stage for contribution claims. In *Theories of change in reality: Strengths, limitations and future directions (A. Koleros, M.-H. Adrien and T. Tyrrell, Eds.), Routledge* (pp. 15 to 24).  
  Published chapter on preparing the ground for contribution claims. Title and book confirmed in PhilArchive; page range from Bohni Nielsen and Lemire (2026).
- Gates, E., and Dyson, L. (2017). Implications of the changing conversation about causality for evaluators. *American Journal of Evaluation*, 38(1), 29 to 46. https://doi.org/10.1177/1098214016644068  
  Generative and other views of causality; CA's place among them.
- Stern, E., Stame, N., Mayne, J., Forss, K., Davies, R., and Befani, B. (2012). *Broadening the range of designs and methods for impact evaluations*. DFID Working Paper 38, Department for International Development. https://www.gov.uk/dfid-research-outputs/dfid-working-paper-38-broadening-the-range-of-designs-and-methods-for-impact-evaluations  
  The DFID paper that set out generative and configurational causation for impact evaluation and gave CA its place among theory-based designs. Befani's appendix on models of causality is widely cited.
- Punton, M., and Welle, K. (2015). *Straws-in-the-wind, hoops and smoking guns: What can process tracing offer to impact evaluation?* CDI Practice Paper 10, Centre for Development Impact, Institute of Development Studies. https://opendocs.ids.ac.uk/opendocs/handle/123456789/5997  
  Plain introduction to the process tracing tests that Mayne (2019, Table 1) folds into CA.
- Douthwaite, B., Mayne, J., McDougall, C., and Paz-Ybarnegaray, R. (2017). Evaluating complex interventions: A theory-driven realist-informed approach. *Evaluation*, 23(3), 294 to 311. https://doi.org/10.1177/1356389017714382  
  CA reasoning combined with realist ideas for complex research-for-development work.
- Paz-Ybarnegaray, R., and Douthwaite, B. (2017). Outcome evidencing: A method for enabling and evaluating program intervention in complex systems. *American Journal of Evaluation*, 38(2), 275 to 293. https://doi.org/10.1177/1098214016676573

## 3. Reviews of CA practice

What published CAs actually do.

- Bohni Nielsen, S., and Lemire, S. (2026). Taking stock of contribution analysis: Reflecting on the past to inform the future. *Canadian Journal of Program Evaluation*, 40(1), 1 to 35. https://doi.org/10.3138/cjpe-2024-0044  
  The most recent systematic review of published CA. Recommends a wider empirical base and quality standards. Its 73-item reference list is the best single map of the field (with a few errors; see errata).
- Buetti, D., Fitzgerald, M., Barber, C., Labelle, P. R., Bourgeois, I., Aubry, T., Cameron, E., and Kendall, C. E. (2025). The use of contribution analysis in evaluating health interventions: A scoping review. *Evaluation & the Health Professions*, 48(3), 363 to 373. https://doi.org/10.1177/01632787241281745  
  Seven health studies (2012 to 2020). All found CA time- and resource-intensive; calls for reporting standards. Online 2024.

## 7. Background on causality

The philosophy behind contributory causes.

- Mackie, J. L. (1974). *The cement of the universe: A study of causation*. Oxford University Press.  
  Source of INUS conditions, which Mayne's contributory cause builds on.
- Cartwright, N., and Hardie, J. (2012). *Evidence-based policy: A practical guide to doing it better*. Oxford University Press.  
  Causal roles and support factors; the logic Mayne uses for generalising (2019, p. 186).
